On 26 September 2022, the DART probe — double asteroid redirection test — crashed straight into the asteroid Dimorphos, to test for the first time whether we could change an asteroid's course. A suicide mission: the probe disintegrated on impact.

Just before the crash, it sent back its last images, the closest ever taken of an asteroid's surface. Those final signals are what the machine draws: the point of collision, the surface coming closer second after second… until the impact.

artistOlfa D'Angelo
signallast images before impact during DART mission
mediumplotter, pigmented ink and india ink on cardboard
series4 drawings
year2025

On 24 December 2021, scientists from Toulouse in France were among the first to receive this signal from Mars. Sped up a hundred times, the recorded vibrations became audible to the human ear.

The drawing holds that unique moment — the first time we could hear the wind blowing on Mars — traced as its wave spectrum, after transformation. Part of tracing sound.

A study of the perception of time passing, through the movement of clouds. Part of lumières, a project on how light — and its changes — shapes our sense of time and space.

artistOlfa D'Angelo
signalpassing clouds
mediumplotter, pigmented india ink on paper
year2024

Light entering a room becomes a metaphor for passing time. The drawings recreate the languor of an afternoon spent looking out of the window. Part of lumières.

The machine captures the variations of light in its surroundings over time and translates them into lines — a trace not mediated by the body.

artistFelix Kuthe
signallight through a window
mediumdrawing machine, acrylic marker on paper
year2024
The machine allows a line that is not mediated by the body. The artist's will stays sovereign — but it acts through the input given to the machine: a light, a smell, a sound, a spectrum of waves… the visible and invisible around us.
